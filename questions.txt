The biological problem presented by the network is the differences between different butterfly species from images. It is a organism-to-organism graph. There are 10 different species of butterflies in the graph. 
Nodes represent buterflies and edges is the visual similarities between them. 
This is calculated by the information extracted from the Leeds butterfly fine-grained species image dataset. 
Network statistics are calculated on edges with weights above the 75-th percentile.
The graph is undirected and is weighted.

Task 3: Individual Graph Analysis (Individual Work) 

Each student must independently select and apply one graph algorithm or graph metric. The selected technique should be motivated and justified by a biological question that can be investigated using the chosen method. Possible choices include, but are not limited to:
• Shortest-path algorithms 
• Minimum spanning trees 
• Topological sorting 
• Connected components  (BFS/DFS) 
• Strongly connected components 
• Community detection algorithms 
• Degree centrality 
• Betweenness centrality 
• Closeness centrality 
• Eigenvector centrality 
• PageRank 
• Link prediction methods 
• Other algorithms available in NetworkX

For the selected algorithm or metric, each student must: 

1. Explain the algorithm or metric. 
2. Describe its objective and computational characteristics. 
3. Justify why it is relevant for the chosen biological network. 
4. Apply it to the dataset. 
5. Interpret the results from both a graph-theoretical and biological perspective. 

The four students in a group must choose different algorithms or metrics.
